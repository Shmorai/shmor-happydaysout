Beach safety for families

A few simple rules make a beach day safer for everyone. Here’s what every family should know before heading to the coast.

By the HappyDaysOut editorial team · 28 August 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Natalya Zaritskaya on Unsplash

Scout’s tip

Scout’s safety rule

Choose a lifeguarded beach and swim between the red and yellow flags. If anyone’s in trouble, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.

  • Never swim at a red flag
  • Keep inflatables for pools
  • Check tide times

Beach flags (UK)

  • Red and yellow: lifeguarded area, the safest place to swim and bodyboard.
  • Black and white chequered: area for surfboards, kayaks and other craft. Don’t swim here.
  • Red: danger. Never go in the water.
  • Orange windsock: offshore winds, so don’t use inflatables.

Rip currents

Rips are strong currents that can drag swimmers out. The RNLI advises: if you’re caught in one, don’t try to swim against it. Swim parallel to the shore if you can, or raise a hand and shout for help. If you’re tired, float to live: lean back and float until you can breathe and swim to safety.

Tides and cliffs

Check tide times before rock pooling or fossil hunting, so you don’t get cut off. Keep away from cliff edges and bases. See beaches and seaside.

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

What should you do if you see someone in trouble in the sea?
In the UK, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard. Don’t go in after them yourself. Throw something that floats if you can.
