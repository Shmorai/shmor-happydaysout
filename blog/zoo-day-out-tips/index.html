Zoo day out tips for families

A zoo day can mean sleepy animals and tired legs, or a brilliant day full of feeding times and close encounters. These tips help you get the good version.

By the HappyDaysOut editorial team · 18 September 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Daiga Ellaby on Unsplash

Scout’s tip

Scout’s zoo routine

Grab a map at the entrance, circle the feeding times and keeper talks, then plan your route around them. Animals are usually most active in the morning.

  • Arrive at opening
  • Pack layers: zoos are mostly outdoors
  • Bring snacks for flagging walkers

On this page

  1. Timing is everything
  2. Little legs
  3. Saving money

Timing is everything

Animals are often livelier in the cooler morning and late afternoon. Feeding times and keeper talks are the highlights for kids, so build your day around them.

Little legs

Big zoos involve a lot of walking. Bring a buggy or carrier for under-5s, plan loops rather than out-and-back routes, and use land trains or buses where the zoo has them.

Saving money

  • Book online in advance.
  • Charity zoos often show a ticket with an optional Gift Aid donation. You can usually choose the standard ticket.
  • Memberships suit local families who visit more than twice a year.
  • Some zoos appear on the rail 2FOR1 list.

Rainy day backup

Many zoos have indoor houses (reptiles, butterflies, nocturnal animals). Or swap to an aquarium. See zoos and aquariums and rainy day ideas.

Frequently asked questions

What time are animals most active at the zoo?
Many animals are more active in the morning and late afternoon, when it’s cooler. Feeding times are also a great time to see them moving.
