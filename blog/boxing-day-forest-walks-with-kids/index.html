Boxing Day forest walks with kids: easy, low-cost family ideas

After a big Christmas Day, a walk in the woods is one of the cheapest and calmest days out going. Forestry England’s own Boxing Day guide lists trails for all ages, from short pushchair-friendly loops to longer circuits. Here is how to pick one that suits your family and avoid the usual festive hiccups.

By the HappyDaysOut editorial team · 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Photo: Seljan Salim on Unsplash

Scout’s tip

Scout’s Boxing Day tip

Pick a short, flat loop and let the children set the pace. Wellies, a flask and a bag of snacks make a bigger difference than a long route.

  • Check the forest’s own page for car park and café opening before you set off
  • Allow extra time: little legs stop for every puddle
  • Pack a spare pair of gloves and socks

Why a forest walk works for Boxing Day

There is no ticket to buy, no queue and no timed slot. Forestry England describes a Boxing Day stroll as a family tradition, and many of its trails are free to walk, although parking charges may apply (members park free, according to its Christmas activity list). That makes it one of the cheapest ways to get children outdoors in the holidays. For more no-cost ideas, see our free days out page.

Short, easy trails for little legs and pushchairs

These come from Forestry England’s Boxing Day walk guide (published December 2025). Details such as length and surfaces are as given there, so check the forest’s page for current conditions:

  • Tall Trees Trail, New Forest, Hampshire: a 2.5km flat trail with a smooth, gravelled surface, passing some of Britain’s oldest Douglas firs and two redwoods over 50m tall.
  • Easy Access Trail, Alice Holt, Surrey: suitable for most wheelchairs and pushchairs, with pink markers and an optional shortcut.
  • Riverside Trail, Hamsterley, County Durham: a short, pushchair-friendly trail beside Bedburn Beck, with Gruffalo sculptures.
  • Ridding Wood Trail, Grizedale, Cumbria: a 1.5km accessible woodland walk with sculptures and unusual trees.
  • Crosscliff View Trail, Dalby, North Yorkshire: short, flat and easy-access, with a viewpoint. Dalby also has a Gruffalo orienteering course and adventure play equipment.
  • Thames Chase, Essex: a 3km trail with a hard-surfaced path, starting and ending near the visitor centre.

Longer walks for older children and teens

If your children have energy to burn, the same guide suggests:

  • Lidcutt Loop, Cardinham, Cornwall: a three-mile walk with a picnic area halfway round. Mobility hire is available on site, with pre-booking recommended.
  • WOW Trail, Whinlatter, Cumbria: a 7km trail through England’s only mountain forest.
  • Ranger’s Path, Sherwood Pines, Nottinghamshire: just over 7km through quieter outer areas of pine and broadleaf woodland.
  • Sculpture Trail, Beechenhurst, Forest of Dean: a circular art walk of about 8km with shortcut options.
  • Westonbirt, The National Arboretum: the Silk Wood walk starts at the Treetop Walkway and is dog friendly.

Teens often enjoy a challenge: turn it into a game with our tips for geocaching, or read days out with teenagers.

Check opening, parking and closures before you go

Do not assume everything is open on 25 and 26 December. A Forestry England notice for Grizedale from 2021 showed the visitor centre and café closed on 24, 25 and 26 December. That is a past example, not 2026 information, so check the specific forest’s page or social channels for this year’s hours, toilets and any weather closures. Parking rules and charges vary by site, so read the forest’s page before you go.

Pack a flask, as cafés may be shut. Our picnic checklist covers what to bring.

Make it a mini adventure

Turn a plain walk into something the children remember: spot the biggest tree, hunt for sculptures, or play I-spy for winter wildlife. Forestry England’s Christmas list also mentions trails such as the Gruffalo sculptures and stargazing on long winter nights, both free apart from parking. If you are hoping for dark skies, our stargazing guide shows where to go. For more ideas on quieter, cheaper winter outings, see November days out and our Christmas days out guide.

Frequently asked questions

Are Forestry England forests open on Boxing Day?
Many woodland trails are open to walkers year-round, but visitor centres, cafés and toilets may close over Christmas. Check the individual forest’s page for this year’s details before you travel.
Is parking free at Forestry England car parks?
Not always. Forestry England says parking charges may apply at its sites, and members park free. Check the forest’s own page for current charges.
Which Boxing Day walks suit pushchairs?
Forestry England’s guide names the Tall Trees Trail in the New Forest, the Easy Access Trail at Alice Holt, the Riverside Trail at Hamsterley and Crosscliff View Trail at Dalby as pushchair-friendly or easy-access options.
What should I take on a family Boxing Day walk?
Waterproofs, wellies, spare gloves and socks, a flask and snacks. Cafés may be closed, and woodland paths can be muddy.
