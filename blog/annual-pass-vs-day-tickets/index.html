Annual pass vs day tickets: when does a pass pay off?

Annual passes can be brilliant value, or an expensive impulse buy. Use this simple method to work out which is right for your family.

By the HappyDaysOut editorial team · 23 September 2026 · Updated 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Ethan Hoover on Unsplash

Scout’s tip

Scout’s break-even rule

Count only the visits you’re genuinely likely to make, on dates the pass actually covers. Be honest: “we’ll go loads” rarely survives a wet February.

  • Use online day prices, not gate prices
  • Include parking and travel
  • Check pre-booking limits

The simple maths

  1. List the visits you’d realistically make in 12 months, and on which dates.
  2. For each visit, add up the online day-ticket price for everyone who’d go.
  3. Add parking if a pass includes it (some premium passes do).
  4. Compare with the total cost of passes for the same people.

If the passes cost less, and the dates you’d go aren’t excluded, the pass pays off. Anything extra is a bonus.

Things that catch families out

  • Exclusion dates: cheaper passes often exclude school holidays and weekends in peak season.
  • Pre-booking: many passes still need you to reserve each visit, with limits on bookings at once.
  • Changes: attractions can be added or removed. For example, Blackpool left the Merlin pass.
  • Monthly plans: may include a joining fee and a minimum term.

Passes worth comparing

  • The Merlin Annual Pass for LEGOLAND, Alton Towers, Thorpe Park, Chessington and more.
  • National Trust or English Heritage memberships.
  • Local zoo memberships, which often pay off in two or three visits.
  • US: the America the Beautiful pass for national parks.

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

How many visits make an annual pass worth it?
It depends on prices, but many passes pay off in two to three visits. Work it out using online day prices for the dates you’d really go.
