Schemes that make days out free

Kids go free

BBC Blue Peter badge

Free child entry at 200+ UK attractions

Children under 16 who earn a Blue Peter badge get free entry at participating attractions when an adult pays and they show their badge and ID card.

Show badge + badge ID card
Merlin attractions stopped taking badges in 2017. Badges can take weeks to arrive. · Checked 14 Oct 2026
How to get onebbc.co.uk ↗
Little ones free

National Trust & English Heritage

Under-5s go free at most properties

Both charities let children under 5 in free at most sites, and family memberships can cover the rest of the gang for a year.

No code needed
Check each property: some events and car parks charge extra. · Checked 14 Oct 2026
Which membership?nationaltrust.org.uk ↗
Free weekend

Bank of America Museums on Us

Free museum entry on the first full weekend of every month

Bank of America, Merrill and Private Bank cardholders get one free general admission at participating museums. The next weekend is 7–8 November 2026.

Show your card + photo ID
Cardholder only, no guests. Special exhibitions excluded. · Checked 14 Oct 2026
Free US days outBank of America ↗
Access offer

Museums for All

Free or reduced entry with an EBT card

Families receiving SNAP benefits can show an EBT card for free or reduced admission at hundreds of participating museums across the US.

Show EBT card + ID
Rules (group size, price) vary by museum. · Checked 14 Oct 2026
Free US days outmuseums4all.org ↗
Free pass

Every Kid Outdoors

Free national parks pass for fourth graders

US fourth graders (and home-schooled 10-year-olds) can get a free pass that covers entrance fees for the family at federal lands. The 2026–27 pass runs to 31 August 2027.

Download the voucher online
Covers entrance fees, not camping or tours. · Checked 14 Oct 2026
How to get iteverykidoutdoors.gov ↗

France’s national museums, including the Louvre, admit under-18s free. EU/EEA residents aged 18–25 usually go free too.

Italian state museums and archaeological sites have opened for free on the first Sunday of the month. Expect crowds and check booking rules.

Free days out in the UK

  • National museums: general admission is free at the UK’s national museums, including the Natural History Museum, Science Museum, British Museum and Young V&A in London, and the national museums of Scotland and Wales.
  • Heritage Open Days: every September, hundreds of buildings in England that normally charge (or are closed) open for free.
  • Beaches, parks and forests: free to enjoy, though car parks may charge.
  • Blue Peter badge: badge holders under 16 get free entry at 200+ attractions.

Free days out in the US

  • Smithsonian museums in Washington, DC are free.
  • Museums on Us: Bank of America cardholders get free museum entry on the first full weekend of each month.
  • Library museum passes: many public libraries lend free or discounted passes to local museums and zoos.
  • National park fee-free days: the National Park Service publishes free-entry days each year. Check nps.gov for the current list.

Free days out in Europe

French national museums admit under-18s free, and Italy’s state museums have opened free on the first Sunday of the month. Many cities also have free parks, playgrounds and festivals. Planning a city break? See city breaks on TravelDeals.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best free days out with kids in the UK?
National museums like the Natural History Museum and Science Museum, beaches, country parks, forests and free festivals. Heritage Open Days in September open many paid buildings for free.
Are London museums really free?
Yes. General admission to the national museums is free. Some special exhibitions charge, and some museums ask you to book a free timed ticket at busy times.
